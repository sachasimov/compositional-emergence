# Individuation and predictive compression — provisional framework

Motivation: atoms, cells, organisms and societies are descriptions at different scales. Are their boundaries merely observer conventions, or do dynamics favor certain partitions? Do not equate these examples or assume universal identity criteria.

For a candidate region or moving support R, let X be its microscopic history, E environmental history, Z=phi(X) an encoded representation, and Y a predeclared future observable (including boundary effects). Compare predictors on identical held-out Y, horizon and context. Use total description cost (model, representation including support/motion, and residual code length); predictive loss alone is not compression. Ideal deterministic full-state dynamics can predict perfectly, so a macro model cannot have more information than the complete state; efficiency depends on coding and computational constraints. Constant targets and trivial fixed points can compress without individuality.

Distinguish token continuity (tracking a process through time), type equivalence (different microstates with similar predictive distributions), and boundary selection. Conditional mutual information I(Y;X|Z,E) measures residual predictive information for a fixed target and sampling distribution; it does not certify causal autonomy. Environmental dependence I(Y;E|Z) is a separate question and observational quantities need not be causal. Use interventions later to examine robustness, not as a synonym for observational prediction.

Objectivity is operational: test agreement across independently specified target sets, coordinate transformations, timescales and detector families, relative to chance and shared assumptions. This cannot prove an observer-free ontology. Distinguish geometric candidates from informational individuals and productive components. Nested units require evidence of cross-scale predictive utility, not just nested boxes.
