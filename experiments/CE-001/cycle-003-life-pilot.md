# Exploratory Life predictive partition pilot v1

Question: does predictive compression distinguish two independently phased nearby oscillators from one coupled region? This is an engineered exact-ensemble sanity check, not blind detection or H4 confirmation. No independent review yet.

Use standard synchronous B3/S23 on a 15x15 torus. Two blinkers centered at (5,7) and (5+d,7) (row,column), d in [3,4,5,6]. Enumerate all four equally probable pairs of horizontal/vertical phases. Evolve 1,2,4 steps. Define each fixed 3x3 window around its center, and their union (nonoverlapping for d>=3). For each region, X is initial full mask; Z is total occupancy; Q is row/column occupancy counts concatenated; Y is future full mask of the same region. Report H(X), H(Z), H(Q), H(Y), H(Y|Z), H(Y|Q), H(Y|X) in bits. Report I(Y_A;Y_B) and number of full-grid trajectories differing from union of the two isolated evolutions, at each distance and horizon. Compare same target across codes; entropy is ideal ensemble cost excluding fixed support and model cost. No fitting, random seeds, or inferred motion. All four phase pairs retained, including destruction. Use a single isolated blinker and isolated glider with all four phases as sanity checks of Life rule (blinker period 2, glider translates by (1,1) after 4). Report all 36 region rows and 12 distance/horizon coupling rows; no threshold tuning. Stop after fixed enumeration. Expectations: at d=6 no coupling, joint future independent, occupancy alone loses phase for isolated blinkers; Q retains phase. At d=3 coupling may invalidate independent prediction; no prespecified direction of entropy change. Fixed supplied regions and phase ensemble are observer assumptions. No claim of discovery, hierarchy, or full MDL coding. Implementation and protocol committed before execution; review required before confirmatory experiments.
