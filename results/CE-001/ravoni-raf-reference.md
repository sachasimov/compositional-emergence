## Exact reference
Food A,B,D,F,I,M; eight reactions transcribed from p.3 (see script). Enumerate every subset, iteratively compute food closure ignoring catalysis, then check all reactants and a catalyst belong to closure. Closed RAF additionally includes every enabled reaction in ambient network. Nonempty convention. Of 256 subsets 8 RAFs and 4 closed RAFs including the maxRAF. Three proper closed RAFs match the three subsets listed in Fig.1 caption/text (the paper repeats R^(2) as a label, and does not separately list the full maxRAF). The maximal constructive food-generated subset is {1,3}. This is a small exhaustive reference oracle, not a replacement for published polynomial maxRAF algorithms.

Construct two copies sharing food places (COIII with S~=F), each nonfood species private; duplicated reactions retain distinct identities. This is an exact structural model of the published operation, excluding inflow/outflow from RAF membership. 65536 subsets enumerated: 80 nonempty RAFs, 16 closed RAFs. Because food is fixed, no cross-private species or catalysts, each restriction is a RAF or empty, independently; hence 9^2-1=80. For closed RAFs, neither copy can be empty: r1 is already enabled by food alone. Hence 4^2=16. Initial author expectation of 24 closed sets (5^2-1) failed the assertion; corrected because empty is not closed here. This failure is explicitly retained in JSON. No new cross-private reaction or novel product type is introduced by this construction. More organizations in the combinatorial lattice do not entail new functional individuals. Actual shared-food kinetics can still couple the copies.

## Exact kinetic counterexample on the published network
Under identical rates and symmetric shared-food COIII, exchanging the copies is a symmetry of the full CTMC generator (when inflows, outflows and initial/delay interventions do not break symmetry). The orbit partition under whole-copy swap is strongly lumpable: permutation maps each transition to equal-rate transition and target orbit to itself. This does not imply summing each species over copies is lumpable (Cardelli et al. 2021 DOI 10.1093/bioinformatics/btab081, existing species-equivalence method; ERODE not executed).

For r4, B+E -> H catalyzed by N, shared B=1, private E1=1,E2=0; compare N1=1,N2=0 vs N1=0,N2=1, other counts zero. Both have identical aggregate counts, but the total catalyzed rate to the aggregate H-increment/B,E-decrement state is lambda_c vs 0 at V=1. Spontaneous r4 contributes lambda_s equally; no other Fig.1 reaction produces H. Thus aggregate rates differ for the same proposed aggregate state, violating strong lumpability for the full marking space. These are admissible markings, not claimed reachable from a particular food-only start. No general statement about weak lumpability or approximate reductions. This is an application of standard lumpability, not a new theorem. Whole-copy symmetry removes labels, not correlations; colocated catalyst/substrate matters. Physical gluing of food places changes the reaction system, unlike observational aggregation of nonfood copies.

## Endpoint limitations
Paper IV uses late mean of M_s=sum over nonfood of 1/(1+x_s), threshold <1, and time to i firings of each transition. The threshold is a proxy, not logical equivalence to persistent joint positivity or functional integration. For 8 species, 7 at count 100 throughout and the eighth alternating between 0 and 100 for equal duration, mean=1/2+7.5/101≈0.5743 despite recurrent absence. If all counts=1 throughout, M_s=4 despite simultaneous presence (not high abundance). These are arithmetic endpoint sanity checks, not simulated trajectories. Use both published endpoints as reference, plus per-species occupancy and catalyzed flux/lineage when separately authorized. Reaction firing counts must specify catalyzed versus spontaneous transitions; censored tau must not be treated as finite slopes.
