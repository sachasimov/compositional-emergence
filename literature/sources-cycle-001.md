## S1 — Rosas (2026), symmetry and abstraction

- Citation: Fernando E. Rosas, *Symmetry as a common origin of hierarchical
  emergence and abstraction*, Science Advances 12(46), eaeh0773 (2026).
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aeh0773).
- Checked primary material: [institutional accepted-version record](https://spiral.imperial.ac.uk/handle/10044/1/150338), abstract.
  It describes dynamical equivariance as a sufficient mechanism for closed
  macroscales and hierarchical beliefs; examples include Hopfield networks and
  Boolean gene regulation. This is not a necessary condition for all emergence
  or a result about collision productivity.
- Metadata check: [publisher-deposited Crossref record](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1126/sciadv.aeh0773)
  confirms title, author, volume, issue, article number, and 2026-11-13 publication.
  Institutional 2026-08-28 is acceptance, not publication.
- **Limit:** publisher returned 403; accepted PDF led to request-a-copy. Final
  proofs are unchecked. The accessible predecessor,
  [arXiv:2512.00984v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.00984v1), §§II.1–II.3,
  discusses closure, equivariance, and subgroup hierarchies. Its title, examples,
  and version differ; do not cite it as the final proof.

## S2 — Rosas et al. (2024), computational closure

- Citation: Fernando E. Rosas, Bernhard C. Geiger, Andrea I. Luppi, Anil K. Seth,
  Daniel Polani, Michael Gastpar, and Pedro A. M. Mediano, *Software in the natural
  world: A computational approach to hierarchical emergence*,
  [arXiv:2402.09090v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.09090v2), revised 2024-06-05.
- Checked: §§III.2–III.5 distinguish informational, causal, and computational
  closure and relate coarse-grainings to computational hierarchies. §IV.1 and
  Figs. 7–8 distinguish trivial constant/invariant descriptions from nontrivial
  computation, with ECA parity examples.
- Scope: descriptions of dynamical processes do not establish equivalence between
  closure and localized productive objects. A constant macrovariable is an
  essential negative control for a closure assay.
- **Limit:** preprint v2 checked; no publication-status claim. A finite-sample
  conditional mutual information estimate needs estimator and history-length
  assumptions before it can approximate the paper's closure conditions.

## S3 — Israeli and Goldenfeld (2006), CA coarse-graining

- Citation: Navot Israeli and Nigel Goldenfeld, *Coarse-graining of cellular
  automata, emergence, and the predictability of complex systems*, Physical
  Review E 73, 026203 (2006).
  [Publisher](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.73.026203);
  [checked preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/nlin/0508033v1).
- Checked: §III, PDF pp. 3–5, gives block projection and a commutativity condition
  for coarse evolution; §IV gives CA applications. Predictability of a projection
  can coexist with difficult microscopic dynamics.
- Scope: a projected lattice description does not by itself identify localized
  objects, interactions, or ancestry. Coarse-grainability is prior art.
- **Limit:** published metadata/abstract and preprint full text checked;
  final-text identity was not established. Do not generalize its finite search
  or theoretical discussion to a verified detector for every rule family.

## S4 — Hanson and Crutchfield (1997), particles and interactions

- Citation: James E. Hanson and James P. Crutchfield, *Computational mechanics of
  cellular automata: An example*, Physica D 103, 169–189 (1997).
  [Author-hosted published PDF](https://csc.ucdavis.edu/~cmg/papers/ECA54.pdf);
  [publisher](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0167-2789(96)00259-X).
- Checked: §§3–4 introduce regular domains, filters, and ECA54 dynamics; §§5–7
  study particles, interactions, statistics, and higher-order structures.
  Introduction, printed p. 170, distinguishes discovering typical patterns from
  engineering a target behavior.
- Scope: particles, collision products, and higher-order patterns are established
  prior work. Its rule-specific construction is a future detector comparator,
  not proof of generalization.
- **Limit:** filters and statistics were not replicated. Whole-grid translational
  recurrence in CE-001 is a different, narrower assay.

## S5 — Shalizi et al. (2006), automatic filters

- Citation: Cosma Rohilla Shalizi, Robert Haslinger, Jean-Baptiste Rouquier,
  Kristina Lisa Klinkner, and Cristopher Moore, *Automatic filters for the detection
  of coherent structure in spatiotemporal systems*, Physical Review E 73, 036104
  (2006). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.73.036104);
  [checked preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/nlin/0508001v1).
- Checked: §II defines local sensitivity with explicit motivation in autonomous
  objects; §III defines local statistical complexity; §IV compares cyclic-CA
  results with an order-parameter approach. §II B/III C give ECA examples.
- Scope: automatic structure filtering and autonomy language predate this project.
  Filter outputs are not automatically validated individuals or multigeneration
  composition evidence.
- **Limit:** preprint checked; final text and algorithms were not replicated.
  Future comparisons need appropriate filters, not only occupancy baselines.

## S6 — Crutchfield (1994), inference and emergence

- Citation: James P. Crutchfield, *The calculi of emergence: computation,
  dynamics and induction*, Physica D 75, 11–54 (1994).
  [Publisher](https://doi.org/10.1016/0167-2789(94)90273-9);
  [checked author manuscript](https://csc.ucdavis.edu/~cmg/papers/CalcEmerg.pdf).
- Checked: Part II §2 distinguishes complexity from randomness and discusses
  metrics; Part III §5.3 treats spatial coherence and distortion in CA pattern
  recognition. Inference resources and model classes affect detected structure.
- Scope: complexity metrics must state the observation/model class. An appealing
  image is insufficient evidence for a hierarchy of physical components.
- **Limit:** the 61-page author manuscript has different pagination from the
  journal. Use named part/section locators rather than journal page guesses.

## S7 — Cook (2004), designed universality

- Citation: Matthew Cook, *Universality in Elementary Cellular Automata*, Complex
  Systems 15(1), 1–40 (2004).
  [Publisher abstract](https://www.complex-systems.com/abstracts/v15_i01_a01/);
  [author record](https://www.paradise.caltech.edu/~cook/papers/), first entry;
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.25088/ComplexSystems.15.1.1).
- Checked: publisher abstract states Rule 110 universality; author record
  describes an intricate construction and supplies year/pages.
- Scope: existence of a construction does not establish its spontaneous
  frequency under a random initialization distribution. This is a logical limit
  of the inference, not a claim that spontaneous computation is impossible.
- **Limit:** publisher PDF returned 404. Detailed construction unchecked;
  no theorem-specific initialization details are asserted here.

## S8 — Hintze and Bohm (2025 preprint), causal replication lineages

- Citation: Arend Hintze and Clifford Bohm, *Rethinking Self-Replication: Detecting
  Distributed Selfhood in the Outlier Cellular Automaton*,
  [arXiv:2508.08047v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08047v1), 2025-08-11.
- Checked: “The Outlier Rule” discloses novelty-search selection of the rule and
  a selected non-extinguishing seed. “Method to Identify and Validate Replicators”
  builds cluster ancestry; “Replicator Classification” reports multigeneration
  replication. “Pattern Emergence in Unbounded Space” discusses boundary effects.
- Scope: ancestry and distributed replication are direct prior art. Rule/seed
  selection and spontaneous downstream outcomes must be distinguished.
  Replication differs from the broader composition endpoint.
- **Limit:** author-reported results unreplicated; data/code listed as available
  upon request. A surfaced [2026 journal route](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44260-026-00074-2)
  failed via authentication redirect; final claims unchecked. Broad “unassisted”
  language in the abstract does not erase selection.
